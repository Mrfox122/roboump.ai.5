---
1-2-3 inning
An inning in which a pitcher faces only three batters and none safely reaches a base. "Three up, three down."
---
1-2-3 double play
A double play in which the pitcher (1) fields a batted ball and throws home to the catcher (2), who retires a runner advancing from third. The catcher then throws to the first baseman (3) to force out the batter. These almost always happen with the bases loaded.
---
20–80 scale
MLB scouts use a 20-80 scale to grade players: 50 is average (MLB level), with 20 being very low and 80 elite. Applies to overall ability & specific skills like hitting or pitching speed.
---
31 move - AKA: 3-1 move or speghetti move, this is a pickoff move where the pitcher steps directly to third base, feints and then throws to first base. It is illegal in all rulebooks where you must throw to third base when stepping directly to third.
---
45-foot line
The 45-foot line in baseball marks the area a batter-runner must stay in to avoid being called out for interfering when running to first base.
---
5-tool player
A position player (non-pitcher) who excels at: hitting for average, hitting for power, base running, throwing, and fielding.
---
A-Ball or Class A
As of the 2022 season, "Class A" is the lowest grouping of modern affiliated minor league baseball, with sub-categories of "High-A" and "Single-A".
---
AA
"Double-A" (AA) is the second-highest level of minor league baseball (below AAA).
---
AAA
"Triple-A" is the highest level of minor league baseball.
---
AAAA player
"Four-A player" is a term for a minor-league player who is consistently successful in the high minor leagues (AAA), but cannot translate that into success at the major-league level.
---
aboard
When a runner is on base. When there are runners safely on base, there are "runners aboard".
---
ace
The best starting pitcher on the team, who is usually first on a pitching rotation.
---
ahead in the count
A term that signifies whether the batter or pitcher possesses the advantage in an at-bat. If a pitcher has thrown more strikes than balls to a batter, the pitcher is ahead in the count; conversely, if the pitcher has thrown more balls than strikes, the batter is ahead.
---
alley
Also "gap" or "power alley", the space between the leftfielder and the centerfielder, or the rightfielder and centerfielder.
---
appeal play
A play in which the defense has an opportunity to gain a favorable ruling from an umpire by addressing a mistake by the offense or seeking the input of another umpire.
---
around the horn
The infielders' practice of throwing the ball to each other after recording an out, provided there are no runners on base.
---
assist
The official scorer awards an assist to every defensive player who fields or touches the ball prior to a putout, even if the contact was unintentional.
---
at bat
A completed plate appearance by a batter which results in a base hit or a non-sacrifice out.
---
B
---
backdoor breaking ball
A breaking pitch, usually a slider, curveball, or cut fastball that, due to its lateral motion, passes through a small part of the strike zone on the outside edge of the plate after seeming as if it would miss the plate entirely.
---
backstop
The fence behind home plate, designed to protect spectators from wild pitches or foul balls.
---
bad hop
A ball that bounces in front of an infielder in an unexpected way, often as a result of imperfections in the playing surface or the spin on the ball.
---
bag
A base. Also, a two-bagger is a double; a three-bagger is a triple; a four-bagger is a home run.
---
balk
An illegal action by the pitcher when there is a runner (or runners) on base. The penalty is that each runner is awarded one base.
---
Baltimore chop
A ball hit forcefully into the ground near home plate, producing a bounce high above the head of a fielder.
---
bandbox
A ballpark with small dimensions that encourages offense, especially home runs.
---
bang bang play
A play in which the runner is barely thrown out, a very close call, typically at first base.
---
base hit
A statistic credited to a batter when such batter reaches base safely on a fair ball that is not the result of an error or fielder's choice.
---
bases loaded
Runners on first, second, and third bases. Also known as "bases full", "bases packed", "bases jammed", or "bases juiced".
---
baserunner
A player on the offensive team who has safely reached base.
---
basket catch
Catching a fly ball with the glove situated about the waistline, as opposed to the hands being situated above the shoulders.
---
bat flip
A celebration in which a batter who just hit a home run flips/tosses the bat in a dramatic way.
---
batter's eye
A solid-colored, usually dark area beyond the center field wall that is the visual backdrop for the batter looking out at the pitcher.
---
battery
The pitcher and catcher considered as a single unit.
---
beanball
A pitch intentionally thrown to hit the batter if he does not move out of the way, especially when directed at the head.
---
benches clear
When a large number of players from both teams leave their respective dugouts to confront each other on the field, usually after a player has been hit by a pitch or a similar provocation.
---
bleachers
Uncovered seats that are typically tiered benches or other inexpensive seats located in the outfield.
---
blooper
A weakly hit fly ball that drops in for a single between an infielder and an outfielder. Also known as a "bloop single", a dying quail, or a duck snort.
---
blow a save
When a relief pitcher enters a game in a save situation but allows the tying run to score.
---
booted
Made an error, kicked it – typically referring to a misplay on a ground ball.
---
breaking ball
Any pitch that markedly deviates from a "straight" or expected path due to a spin used by the pitcher. Examples are the curveball, the slider and the screwball.
---
brushback pitch
A pitch intentionally thrown close to a batter to intimidate him, i.e., to "brush him back" from the plate.
---
bullpen
The area used by pitchers and catchers to warm up. It can also refer to a team's relief pitching corps.
---
bunt
To deliberately bat the ball weakly to a particular spot on the infield by holding the bat nearly still and letting the ball hit it.
---
C
---
can of corn
A high, easy-to-catch, fly ball hit to the outfield.
---
caught looking
A term used when the third strike is called on a batter without the batter attempting to swing at the pitch.
---
cellar
Last place, bottom of the standings. Also basement.
---
changeup
A pitch meant to look like a fastball but with less velocity, short for change of pace.
---
cheese
A fastball, particularly one that is difficult to hit.
---
chin music
A high and tight, up and in pitch meant to knock a batter back from home plate.
---
chopper
A batted ball that immediately strikes the hardened area of dirt directly in front of home plate, causing it to bounce high into the infield.
---
choke up
When a batter slides his hands up from the knob end of the bat to give him more control.
---
clean inning
When a pitcher allows zero hits, walks or runs and the defense does not commit an error in an inning.
---
cleanup hitter
The fourth batter in the lineup, usually a power hitter.
---
closer
A relief pitcher who is consistently used to "close" or finish a game by getting the final outs.
---
clutch
Good performance under pressure when good performance really matters.
---
complete game
The act of a pitcher pitching an entire game himself, without the benefit of a relief pitcher.
---
contact hitter
A hitter who does not strike out often and is usually able to make contact with the ball and put it in play.
---
count
The number of balls and strikes a batter has in his current at-bat.
---
curveball
A pitch that curves or breaks from a straight or expected flight path toward home plate.
---
cutter
A cut fastball or cutter is a fastball that has lateral movement, similar to a slider but with more speed.
---
cycle
When a given player hits a single, double, triple and home run in the same game.
---
D
---
dead-ball era
The period between 1903 and 1918, when the composition of the baseball tended to limit offense.
---
designated hitter
A player who permanently hits in the place of a defensive player (typically the pitcher). Abbreviated as DH.
---
dinger
A home run.
---
double
A hit where the batter makes it safely to second base.
---
double play
A play by the defense where two offensive players are put out as a result of continuous action.
---
drag bunt
A bunt in which a left-handed hitter lays down a bunt out of the reach of the pitcher and toward the right side of the infield.
---
dugout
The area where a team's bench is located, typically slightly depressed below field level.
---
E
---
earned run
Any run for which the pitcher is held accountable (i.e., the run did not score as a result of a fielding error or a passed ball).
---
Eephus pitch
A very slow pitch with a high arcing trajectory, intended to fool a hitter's timing.
---
error
A fielder's misplay which allows a batter or baserunner to reach one or more additional bases when that advance could have been prevented by ordinary effort.
---
F
---
fan
To strike out a batter, especially on a swinging third strike.
---
farm system
A team's network of affiliated minor league teams for the purpose of player development.
---
fastball
A pitch that is thrown more for high velocity than for movement; it is the most common type of pitch.
---
fielder's choice
The act of a fielder, upon fielding a batted ball, choosing to try to putout a baserunner instead of the batter-runner.
---
fireman
A team's top relief pitcher who is often brought in to end an offensive rally and "put out the fire".
---
fly ball
A ball hit high in the air.
---
force play
Any play where a runner is forced to advance because the batter has become a runner.
---
foul ball
A batted ball that settles into foul territory.
---
foul tip
A batted ball that goes sharp and direct from the bat to the catcher's mitt and is legally caught. It is a strike.
---
full count
A count of 3 balls and 2 strikes.
---
G
---
gap
The space between outfielders. Also alley.
---
golden sombrero
When a batter strikes out four times in one game.
---
goose egg
When a team has zero on the scoreboard for an inning.
---
grand slam
A home run hit with the bases loaded, scoring four runs.
---
Green Monster
The popular nickname for the high left field wall at Fenway Park in Boston.
---
Ground-rule Double
when a fair ball bounces in play and then goes over or under the outfield fence, or gets stuck in the fence, or goes through or under a scoreboard. The result of this is a double due to dead ball base awards.

H
---
hat trick
To strike out three times in one game.
---
heat
A fastball. Also heater.
---
hit and run
An offensive tactic where a baserunner starts running as if to steal and the batter is obligated to swing at the pitch.
---
homer
A home run.
---
hot corner
The area around third base, so called because right-handed batters tend to hit hard line drives down the third base line.
---
I
---
immaculate inning
A half-inning in which the pitcher strikes out all three batters he faces with exactly nine pitches.
---
infield fly rule
The umpire calls the batter out when the batter hits a fly ball that can be caught with ordinary effort by an infielder in fair territory when there are runners on first and second, or on first, second, and third, with less than two outs.
---
inherited runner
The runner or runners on base when a relief pitcher enters the game.
---
inside-the-park home run
A play where a hitter scores a home run without hitting the ball out of play.
---
intentional walk
When the defensive team deliberately walks a batter.
---
interference
An infraction where a person illegally changes the course of play from what is expected.
---
J
---
jam
When a pitcher pitches inside to the batter, making it difficult to swing. Also when a pitcher is in a difficult situation with runners in scoring position.
---
junk
Breaking balls and knuckleballs, pitches that are hard to hit due to movement rather than velocity.
---
K
---
K
The traditional abbreviation for a strikeout. A backwards K is often used to denote a called strikeout.
---
knuckleball
A pitch thrown with no spin, which tends to flutter and move erratically on its way to the plate.
---
L
---
laser show
A batting performance with a high number of base hits, particularly line drives.
---
leadoff hitter
The first batter in the lineup.
---
left on base
A baserunner is left on base (LOB) when the half-inning ends and he has not scored or been put out.
---
line drive
A batted ball that is hit hard in the air and has a low arc. Also liner.
---
M
---
Mendoza line
A batting average of .200.
---
middle reliever
A relief pitcher who is brought in typically during the middle-innings (4, 5, and 6).
---
mound
The pitcher's mound.
---
N
---
no-hitter
A game in which one team does not get any hits. Also no-no.
---
O
---
obstruction
When a fielder illegally hinders a baserunner.
---
on-deck
The next batter due to bat after the current batter.
---
opener
A traditional relief pitcher who starts a game for strategic reasons and is replaced early.
---
opposite field
A hit to the "opposite" side of the field from the direction of a player's natural swing.
---
out pitch
The type of pitch that a pitcher relies on to get an out, often his best pitch.
---
P
---
painting the corners
When a pitcher throws pitches that just touch the edge of the strike zone.
---
passed ball
A catcher is charged with a passed ball when he fails to hold or control a pitch which should have been handled with ordinary effort, allowing a runner to advance.
---
payoff pitch
A pitch thrown with a full count.
---
perfect game
A no-hitter where no batter reaches base by any means (walk, error, etc.). "27 up, 27 down".
---
pickle
A rundown.
---
pickoff
A quick throw from the pitcher or catcher to a fielder covering a base to catch a runner off guard.
---
pinch hitter
A substitute batter.
---
pinch runner
A substitute baserunner.
---
pine tar
A sticky substance used by batters to improve their grip on the bat.
---
pitchout
A defensive tactic where the pitch is thrown outside so the catcher can quickly throw to a base to pick off a runner.
---
platoon
The practice of assigning two players to the same position, often based on their ability to hit against left-handed vs. right-handed pitchers.
---
plunked
Hit by a pitch.
---
pull hitter
A batter who generally hits the ball to the same side of the field as which side he bats from.
---
Punch and Judy hitter
A hitter who has very little power.
---
purpose pitch
A brushback pitch, intended to make the batter move away from home plate.
---
Q
---
quality start
When a starting pitcher pitches at least six complete innings and allows three or fewer earned runs.
---
quick pitch
An illegal pitch where the ball is thrown before the batter is reasonably set in the batter's box.
---
R
---
rally
To come back from a deficit, typically in the late innings.
---
ribbie
Slang for a run batted in (RBI).
---
rope
A hard-hit line drive. Also frozen rope.
---
rubber arm
A pitcher who can throw many pitches without tiring.
---
rundown
A play in which a runner is stranded between two bases and runs back and forth to avoid being tagged out.
---
S
---
sabermetrics
The analysis of baseball through objective evidence, especially statistics.
---
sacrifice bunt
A bunt that allows a runner on base to advance while the batter is put out.
---
sacrifice fly
A fly ball hit to the outfield which is caught for an out, but allows a runner to score from 3rd base.
---
save
A statistic credited to a relief pitcher who successfully maintains a lead until the end of the game.
---
seeing-eye single
A ground ball that just eludes capture by an infielder, as if it could "see" where it needed to go.
---
setup man
A relief pitcher who is consistently used immediately before the closer.
---
seventh-inning stretch
The period between the top and bottom of the seventh inning, when fans traditionally stand up and stretch.
---
shutout
When a team prevents its opponent from scoring any runs in a game.
---
side retired
When the third out of an inning is called.
---
single
A one-base hit.
---
sinker
A pitch, typically a fastball, that breaks sharply downward as it crosses the plate.
---
slap hitter
A hitter who sacrifices power for batting average, trying to make contact with the ball.
---
slide
When a runner drops to the ground when running toward a base to avoid a tag.
---
slider
A relatively fast pitch with a slight curve in the opposite direction of the throwing arm.
---
slump
An extended period when a player or team is not performing well.
---
small ball
An offensive strategy that focuses on teamwork and good execution, centering on tactics like walks, base hits, bunts, and stolen bases.
---
southpaw
A left-handed player, especially a pitcher.
---
spitball
A pitch in which the ball has been altered by the application of spit or some other foreign substance.
---
squeeze play
A tactic used to attempt to score a runner from third on a bunt.
---
steal
To advance to the next base while the pitcher is delivering the ball to home plate.
---
strikeout
When a pitcher throws three strikes in one plate appearance, retiring the batter.
---
strike zone
An area above home plate with defined upper and lower limits through which a pitch must pass to be called a strike.
---
sweet spot
The area on the barrel of the bat where a batter tries to make contact with the ball for optimal results.
---
switch hitter
A batter who can hit from either side of the plate (left-handed and right-handed).
---
T
---
tag
A tag out. A runner is out if, while in jeopardy, a fielder touches him with a live ball or the hand or glove holding a live ball.
---
tag up
When a batter hits a fly ball that is caught, every runner must retreat back to the base he just left. Once he has touched that base, he may legally advance again.
---
tater
A home run.
---
Texas Leaguer
A weakly hit fly ball that drops in for a single between an infielder and an outfielder.
---
three true outcomes
The three ways a plate appearance can end without fielders coming into play: walks, home runs, and strikeouts.
---
triple
A three-base hit.
---
triple crown
When a batter leads the league in home runs, runs batted in, and batting average, or a pitcher leads the league in earned run average, wins, and strikeouts.
---
twin bill
A doubleheader.
---
U
---
umpire
The official in charge of a game.
---
utility player
A player who can play several different positions.
---
V
---
VORP
Value Over Replacement Player, a sabermetric method of evaluating players.
---
vulture
A reliever who records wins in late innings by being the pitcher of record in the midst of a comeback.
---
W
---
walk
A base on balls.
---
walk-off
When the home team immediately wins the game by scoring a run to take the lead in the bottom of the last inning.
---
warning track
The dirt and finely-ground gravel area along the outfield fence.
---
wheelhouse
A hitter's power zone, usually a pitch waist-high and over the heart of the plate.
---
whiff
A swinging strike.
---
wild pitch
A pitch that is too high, too low, or too wide of home plate for the catcher to catch with ordinary effort, allowing a runner to advance.
---
windup
One of the two legal pitching positions.
---
Y
---
yacker
A curveball with a big break.
---
yard
The baseball field. A home run has "left the yard".
---
Z
---
zone
The strike zone.
