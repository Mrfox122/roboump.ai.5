---
1.00 – OBJECTIVES OF THE GAME
---
1.01 Baseball is a game between two teams of nine players each, under direction of a manager, played on an enclosed field in accordance with these rules, under jurisdiction of one or more umpires.
---
1.02 The offensive team’s objective is to have its batter become a runner, and its runners advance.
---
1.03 The defensive team’s objective is to prevent offensive players from becoming runners, and to prevent their advance around the bases.
---
1.04 When a batter becomes a runner and touches all bases legally he shall score one run for his team.
---
1.05 The objective of each team is to win by scoring more runs than the opponent.
---
1.06 The winner of the game shall be that team which shall have scored, in accordance with these rules, the greater number of runs at the conclusion of a regulation game.
---
2.00 – THE PLAYING FIELD
---
2.01 Layout of the Field
The field shall be laid out according to the instructions below, supplemented by the diagrams in the appendices. The infield shall be a 90-foot square. The outfield shall be the area between two foul lines formed by extending two sides of the square. The distance from home base to the nearest fence, stand or other obstruction on fair territory shall be 250 feet or more. A distance of 320 feet or more along the foul lines, and 400 feet or more to center field is preferable. The infield shall be graded so that the base lines and home plate are level. The pitcher’s plate shall be 10 inches above the level of home plate. The degree of slope from a point 6 inches in front of the pitcher’s plate to a point 6 feet toward home plate shall be 1 inch to 1 foot, and such degree of slope shall be uniform. The infield and outfield, including the boundary lines, are fair territory and all other area is foul territory. It is desirable that the line from home base through the pitcher’s plate to second base shall run East-Northeast. It is recommended that the distance from home base to the backstop, and from the base lines to the nearest fence, stand or other obstruction on foul territory shall be 60 feet or more.
---
2.02 Home Base
Home base shall be marked by a five-sided slab of whitened rubber. It shall be a 17-inch square with two of the corners removed so that one edge is 17 inches long, two adjacent sides are 8.5 inches and the remaining two sides are 12 inches and set at an angle to make a point. It shall be set in the ground with the point at the intersection of the lines extending from home base to first base and to third base; with the 17-inch edge facing the pitcher’s plate, and the two 12-inch edges coinciding with the first and third base lines. The top edges of home base shall be beveled and the base shall be fixed in the ground level with the ground surface.
---
2.03 The Bases
First, second and third bases shall be marked by white canvas or rubber-covered bags, securely attached to the ground. The first and third base bags shall be entirely within the infield. The second base bag shall be centered on second base. The bags shall be 18 inches square, not less than three nor more than five inches thick, and filled with soft material.
---
2.04 The Pitcher’s Plate
The pitcher’s plate shall be a rectangular slab of whitened rubber, 24 inches by 6 inches. It shall be set in the ground so that the distance between the pitcher’s plate and home base (the rear point of home plate) shall be 60 feet, 6 inches.
---
2.05 Benches
The home Club shall furnish players’ benches, one each for the home and visiting teams. Such benches shall not be less than twenty-five feet from the base lines. They shall be roofed and shall be enclosed at the back and ends.
---
3.00 – EQUIPMENT AND UNIFORMS
---
3.01 The Ball
The ball shall be a sphere formed by yarn wound around a small core of cork, rubber or similar material, covered with two strips of white horsehide or cowhide, tightly stitched together. It shall weigh not less than five nor more than 5.25 ounces avoirdupois and measure not less than nine nor more than 9.25 inches in circumference. No player shall intentionally discolor or damage the ball by rubbing it with soil, rosin, paraffin, licorice, sand-paper, emery-paper or other foreign substance. PENALTY: The umpire shall demand the ball and remove the offender from the game. In addition, the offender shall be suspended automatically for 10 games.
---
3.02 The Bat
(a) The bat shall be a smooth, round stick not more than 2.61 inches in diameter at the thickest part and not more than 42 inches in length. The bat shall be one piece of solid wood. (b) Cupped Bats. An indentation in the end of the bat up to 1.25 inches in depth is permitted and may be no wider than two inches and no less than one inch in diameter. The indentation must be curved with no foreign substance added. (c) The bat handle, for not more than 18 inches from its end, may be covered or treated with any material or substance to improve the grip. Any such material or substance that extends past the 18-inch limitation shall cause the bat to be removed from the game. (d) No colored bat may be used in a professional game unless approved by Major League Baseball.
---
3.03 Player Uniforms
(a) All players on a team shall wear uniforms identical in color, trim and style, and all players’ uniforms shall include minimal six-inch numbers on their backs. (b) Any part of an undershirt exposed to view shall be of a uniform solid color for all players on a team. (c) No player whose uniform does not conform to that of his teammates shall be permitted to participate in a game. (d) Sleeve lengths may vary for individual players, but the sleeves of each individual player shall be approximately the same length; no player shall wear ragged, frayed or slit sleeves; and no pitcher shall wear sleeves that are white, gray, nor, in the judgment of an umpire, distracting in any manner. (e) No player shall attach to his uniform tape or other material of different color from his uniform.
---
3.04 Catcher’s Mitt
The catcher may wear a leather mitt not more than thirty-eight inches in circumference, nor more than fifteen and one-half inches from top to bottom. The space between the thumb section and the finger section of the mitt shall not exceed six inches at the top of the mitt and four inches at the base of the thumb crotch. The web shall measure not more than seven inches across the top or more than six inches from its top to the base of the thumb crotch.
---
3.05 First Baseman’s Glove
The first baseman may wear a leather glove or mitt not more than thirteen inches long from top to bottom and not more than eight inches wide across the palm. The space between the thumb section and the finger section shall not exceed four inches at the top of the mitt and three and one-half inches at the base of the thumb crotch. The web of the mitt shall measure not more than five inches from its top to the base of the thumb crotch.
---
3.06 Fielding Gloves
Each fielder, other than the catcher, may use or wear a leather glove. The glove shall not measure more than 13 inches from the tip of any one of the 4 fingers, through the ball pocket to the bottom edge or heel of glove. The glove shall not measure more than 7.75 inches wide. The crotch opening shall measure not more than 4.5 inches at the top, not more than 5.75 inches deep, and shall be 3.5 inches wide at its bottom.
---
3.07 Pitcher’s Glove
(a) The pitcher’s glove may not, exclusive of piping, be white, gray, nor, in the judgment of an umpire, distracting in any manner. (b) No pitcher shall attach to his glove any foreign material of a color different from the glove.
---
3.08 Helmets
(a) All players shall use some type of protective helmet while at bat and while running the bases. (b) All catchers shall wear a catcher’s protective helmet and face mask while receiving a pitch. (c) All base coaches shall wear a protective helmet while performing their duties. (d) All bat/ball boys or girls shall wear a double ear-flap protective helmet while performing their duties.
---
4.00 – GAME PRELIMINARIES
---
4.01 Umpire Duties
Before the game begins the umpire shall: (a) Require strict observance of all rules governing implements of play and equipment of players; (b) Be sure that all playing lines are marked with a white material; (c) Receive from the home Club a supply of regulation baseballs. The umpire shall be the sole judge of the fitness of the balls to be used in the game; (d) Be assured by the home Club that at least one dozen regulation reserve balls are immediately available; (e) Have in his possession at least two alternate balls.
---
4.03 Exchange of Lineup Cards
The umpire shall receive each team's batting order in duplicate. The umpire-in-chief shall make certain that the original and copies of the respective batting orders are identical, and then tender a copy of each batting order to the opposing manager. The copy retained by the umpire shall be the official batting order. As soon as the home team’s batting order is handed to the umpire-in-chief the umpires are in charge of the playing field.
---
5.00 – PLAYING THE GAME
---
5.01 Starting the Game (“Play Ball!”)
(a) At the time set for beginning the game the players of the home team shall take their defensive positions, the first batter of the visiting team shall take his position in the batter’s box, the umpire-in-chief shall call “Play,” and the game shall start. (b) After the umpire calls “Play” the ball is alive and in play. (c) The pitcher shall deliver the pitch to the batter.
---
5.02 Fielding Positions
When the ball is put in play, all fielders other than the catcher shall be on fair territory. (a) The catcher shall station himself directly back of the plate. (b) The pitcher shall take his legal position. (c) Infielder Positioning: The defensive team must have a minimum of four players with both feet completely in front of the outer boundary of the infield dirt, with at least two infielders positioned on each side of second base.
---
5.03 Base Coaches
(a) The team at bat shall station two base coaches on the field, one near first base and one near third base. (b) Base coaches shall be limited to two in number and shall be in team uniform. (c) Base coaches must remain within the coach’s box.
---
5.04 Batting
(a) Batting Order: Each player of the offensive team shall bat in the order that his name appears in his team’s batting order. (b) The Batter’s Box: The batter shall take his position in the batter’s box promptly. The batter shall not leave his position in the batter’s box after the pitcher comes to Set Position, or starts his windup. (c) Completing Time at Bat: A batter has legally completed his time at bat when he is put out or becomes a runner.
---
5.05 When the Batter Becomes a Runner
(a) The batter becomes a runner when: (1) He hits a fair ball; (2) The third strike is not caught, providing first base is unoccupied, or first base is occupied with two out; (3) The catcher or any fielder interferes with him; (4) A fair ball touches an umpire or a runner on fair territory before touching a fielder. (b) The batter becomes a runner and is entitled to first base without liability to be put out when: (1) Four “balls” have been called by the umpire; (2) He is touched by a pitched ball which he is not attempting to hit.
---
5.06 Running the Bases
(a) Occupying the Base: A runner acquires the right to an unoccupied base when he touches it before he is out. Two runners may not occupy a base. (b) Advancing Bases: In advancing, a runner shall touch first, second, third and home base in order. Each runner may advance one base without liability to be put out on a balk. (c) Dead Balls: The ball becomes dead and runners advance one base, or return to their bases, without liability to be put out, when a pitched ball touches a batter, the plate umpire interferes with a catcher's throw, a balk is committed, or a foul ball is not caught, among other reasons.
---
5.07 Pitching
(a) Legal Pitching Delivery: There are two legal pitching positions, the Windup Position and the Set Position. Pitchers shall take signs from the catcher while in contact with the pitcher’s plate. (b) Warm-Up Pitches: When a pitcher takes his position at the beginning of each inning, or when he relieves another pitcher, he shall be permitted to pitch preparatory pitches. (c) Pitcher Delays: When the bases are unoccupied, the pitcher shall deliver the ball to the batter within 12 seconds after he receives the ball.
---
5.08 How a Team Scores
(a) One run shall be scored each time a runner legally advances to and touches first, second, third and home base before three men are put out to end the inning. EXCEPTION: A run is not scored if the runner advances to home base during a play in which the third out is made by the batter-runner before he touches first base, by any runner being forced out, or by a preceding runner who is declared out because he failed to touch one of the bases.
---
5.09 Making an Out
(a) Retiring the Batter: A batter is out when his fair or foul fly ball is legally caught, a third strike is legally caught by the catcher, he bunts foul on third strike, or an Infield Fly is declared, among other reasons. (b) Retiring a Runner: Any runner is out when he runs more than three feet away from his base path to avoid being tagged, he is tagged when the ball is alive while off his base, or he passes a preceding runner before such runner is out, among other reasons. (c) Appeal Plays: Any runner shall be called out, on appeal, when after a fly ball is caught, he fails to retouch his original base before he or his original base is tagged.
---
5.10 Substitutions and Pitching Changes
(a) A player, or players, may be substituted during a game at any time the ball is dead. (b) The manager shall immediately notify the umpire-in-chief of any substitution. (c) A player once removed from a game shall not re-enter that game. (d) The pitcher named in the batting order shall pitch to the first batter until such batter is put out or reaches first base, unless the pitcher sustains injury or illness.
---
5.11 Designated Hitter Rule
A hitter may be designated to bat for the starting pitcher and all subsequent pitchers in any game without otherwise affecting the status of the pitcher(s) in the game.
---
5.12 Calling “Time” and Dead Balls
(a) When an umpire suspends play, he shall call “Time.” At the umpire-in-chief’s call of “Play,” the suspension is lifted and play resumes. Between the call of “Time” and the call of “Play” the ball is dead.
---
6.00 – IMPROPER PLAY, ILLEGAL ACTION, AND MISCONDUCT
---
6.01 Interference, Obstruction, and Catcher Collisions
(a) Batter or Runner Interference: It is interference by a batter or a runner when they hinder a fielder attempting to make a play, intentionally deflect a foul ball, or gather around a base to confuse fielders, among other actions. (b) Fielder Right of Way: The players, coaches or any member of a team at bat shall vacate any space needed by a fielder who is attempting to field a batted or thrown ball. (c) Catcher Interference: The batter becomes a runner and is entitled to first base if the catcher or any fielder interferes with him. (d) Unintentional Interference: In case of unintentional interference with play by any person authorized to be on the playing field the ball is alive and in play. (e) Spectator Interference: When there is spectator interference with any thrown or batted ball, the ball shall be dead and penalties imposed to nullify the act. (f) Coach and Umpire Interference: If a thrown ball accidentally touches a base coach, or a pitched or thrown ball touches an umpire, the ball is alive and in play. (g) Obstruction: When obstruction occurs, the umpire shall call or signal “Obstruction.” (h) Collisions at Home Plate: A runner attempting to score may not deviate from his direct pathway to the plate in order to initiate contact with the catcher. Unless the catcher is in possession of the ball, the catcher cannot block the pathway of the runner. (i) Sliding to Bases on Double Play Attempts: If a runner does not engage in a bona fide slide, and initiates contact with the fielder for the purpose of breaking up a double play, he should be called for interference.
---
6.02 Pitcher Illegal Action
(a) Balks: If there is a runner, or runners, it is a balk for violations such as the pitcher failing to deliver the pitch after making a motion, failing to step directly toward a base before throwing, or making an illegal pitch. (b) Illegal Pitches With Bases Unoccupied: If the pitcher makes an illegal pitch with the bases unoccupied, it shall be called a ball. (c) Pitching Prohibitions: The pitcher shall not bring his pitching hand in contact with his mouth or lips in the 18-foot circle, apply a foreign substance to the ball, or intentionally pitch at the batter.
---
6.03 Batter Illegal Action
(a) A batter is out for illegal action when he hits a ball with one or both feet on the ground entirely outside the batter’s box, steps from one batter’s box to the other while the pitcher is in position, or interferes with the catcher’s fielding or throwing. (b) Batting Out of Turn: A batter shall be called out, on appeal, when he fails to bat in his proper turn, and another batter completes a time at bat in his place.
---
6.04 Unsportsmanlike Conduct
(a) No manager, player, coach, or other team personnel shall at any time incite a demonstration by spectators, use language which will reflect upon opposing players or an umpire, or make intentional contact with an umpire. (d) When a manager, player, coach or trainer is ejected from a game, he shall leave the field immediately.
---
7.00 – ENDING THE GAME
---
7.01 Regulation Games
(a) A regulation game consists of nine innings, unless extended because of a tie score, or shortened. (b) Extra Innings: If the score is tied after nine completed innings, play shall continue. Each half-inning after the ninth will begin with a runner on second base. (c) A game may be called for reasons such as weather or lighting failure. (d) A called game is a regulation game if five innings have been completed.
---
7.02 Suspended Games
A game that is called prior to becoming a regulation game, or when the game is tied, must be scheduled to be resumed. A suspended game shall be resumed at the exact point of suspension.
---
7.03 Forfeited Games
A game may be forfeited to the opposing team when a team fails to appear, refuses to start or continue play, or willfully violates the rules after a warning.
---
7.04 Protesting Games
Protesting a game shall never be permitted, regardless of whether the complaint is based on umpire judgment or misapplication of the rules.
---
8.00 – THE UMPIRE
---
8.01 Umpire Qualifications and Authority
(a) The Office of the Commissioner shall appoint umpires for each league championship game. (b) Each umpire is a representative of the league and professional baseball and is authorized to enforce all rules. (c) Each umpire has the authority to disqualify any player, coach, or manager for unsportsmanlike conduct.
---
8.02 Appeal of Umpire Decisions
(a) Any umpire’s decision involving judgment, such as whether a pitch is a strike or a ball, is final. (b) If there is reasonable doubt that a decision conflicts with the rules, the manager may appeal to the umpire who made the decision. (c) The umpire making the decision may ask another umpire for information before making a final decision.
---
9.00 – THE OFFICIAL SCORER
---
9.01 Official Scorer (General Rules)
(a) The Office of the Commissioner shall appoint an Official Scorer for each game who has the sole authority to make all decisions involving judgment, such as whether a batter's advance is a hit or an error.
---
9.02 Official Scorer Report
The official score report shall include records for each batter and runner, each fielder, and each pitcher, along with other game data.
---
9.04 Runs Batted In
A run batted in (RBI) is credited to a batter whose action at bat causes one or more runs to score.
---
9.05 Base Hits
A base hit is credited to a batter when they reach base safely on a fair ball that is not the result of an error or fielder's choice.
---
9.06 Determining Value of Base Hits
A base hit is scored as a one-base hit, two-base hit, three-base hit, or home run depending on the base the batter safely reaches.
---
9.07 Stolen Bases and Caught Stealing
A stolen base is credited to a runner who advances one base unaided by other actions. A runner is charged as "caught stealing" if they are put out while trying to steal.
---
9.08 Sacrifices
A sacrifice bunt or sacrifice fly is scored when, before two are out, a batter's action advances one or more runners.
---
9.09 Putouts
A putout is credited to a fielder whose action causes the out of a batter-runner or runner.
---
9.10 Assists
An assist is credited to a fielder who throws or deflects a ball in such a way that a putout results.
---
9.12 Errors
An error is charged against a fielder whose misplay prolongs a batter's time at bat or allows a runner to advance.
---
9.13 Wild Pitches and Passed Balls
A pitcher is charged with a wild pitch when a pitch is so high, wide, or low that the catcher cannot control it with ordinary effort, allowing a runner to advance. A catcher is charged with a passed ball when they fail to hold a legally pitched ball that should have been handled, allowing a runner to advance.
---
9.15 Strikeouts
A strikeout is credited to a pitcher and charged to a batter when the umpire calls three strikes.
---
9.16 Earned Runs and Runs Allowed
An earned run is a run for which a pitcher is held accountable, determined by reconstructing the inning without errors and passed balls.
---
9.17 Winning and Losing Pitcher
The winning pitcher is the one whose team assumes a lead while they are in the game and does not relinquish it. The losing pitcher is responsible for the run that gives the winning team a lead it never relinquishes.
---
9.19 Saves for Relief Pitchers
A save is credited to a relief pitcher who is the finishing pitcher in a game won by his team, is not the winning pitcher, and meets certain conditions.
---
DEFINITIONS OF TERMS
---
ADJUDGED is a judgment decision by the umpire.
---
An APPEAL is the act of a fielder in claiming violation of the rules by the offensive team.
---
A BALK is an illegal act by the pitcher with a runner or runners on base, entitling all runners to advance one base.
---
A BALL is a pitch which does not enter the strike zone in flight and is not struck at by the batter.
---
A BASE ON BALLS is an award of first base granted to a batter who receives four pitches outside the strike zone.
---
A BUNT is a batted ball not swung at, but intentionally met with the bat and tapped slowly within the infield.
---
A CATCH is the act of a fielder in getting secure possession in his hand or glove of a ball in flight and firmly holding it.
---
A DOUBLE PLAY is a play by the defense in which two offensive players are put out as a result of continuous action, providing there is no error between putouts.
---
A FAIR BALL is a batted ball that settles on fair ground between home and first base, or between home and third base, or that is on or over fair territory when bounding to the outfield past first or third base.
---
A FIELDER’S CHOICE is the act of a fielder who handles a fair grounder and, instead of throwing to first base to put out the batter-runner, throws to another base in an attempt to put out a preceding runner.
---
A FORCE PLAY is a play in which a runner legally loses his right to occupy a base by reason of the batter becoming a runner.
---
A FOUL BALL is a batted ball that settles on foul territory.
---
A FOUL TIP is a batted ball that goes sharp and direct from the bat to the catcher and is legally caught.
---
An INFIELD FLY is a fair fly ball (not including a line drive nor an attempted bunt) which can be caught by an infielder with ordinary effort, when first and second, or first, second and third bases are occupied, before two are out.
---
INTERFERENCE is an act by the team at bat which interferes with, obstructs, impedes, hinders or confuses any fielder attempting to make a play.
---
OBSTRUCTION is the act of a fielder who, while not in possession of the ball and not in the act of fielding the ball, impedes the progress of any runner.
---
A STRIKE is a legal pitch when so called by the umpire, which is struck at and missed, not struck at if it passes through the strike zone, fouled with less than two strikes, or becomes a foul tip.
---
The STRIKE ZONE is that area over home plate the upper limit of which is a horizontal line at the midpoint between the top of the shoulders and the top of the uniform pants, and the lower level is a line at the hollow beneath the kneecap.
---
A TAG is the action of a fielder in touching a base with his body while holding the ball securely, or touching a runner with the ball or with his hand or glove holding the ball.
---
A THROW is the act of propelling the ball with the hand and arm to a given objective and is to be distinguished, always, from the pitch
---
A TIE GAME is a regulation game which is called when each team has the same number of runs.
---
“TIME” is the announcement by an umpire of a legal interruption of play, during which the ball is dead
---
TOUCH. To touch a player or umpire is to touch any part of his body, or any uniform or equipment worn by him (but not any jewelry (e.g., necklaces, bracelets, etc.) worn by a player). (Touch) Comment: Equipment shall be considered worn by a player or umpire if it is in contact with its intended place on his person.
---
A TRIPLE PLAY is a play by the defense in which three offensive players are put out as a result of continuous action, providing there is no error between putouts
---
WILD PITCH is one so high, so low, or so wide of the plate that it cannot be handled with ordinary effort by the catcher.
---
WIND-UP POSITION is one of the two legal pitching positions
---
